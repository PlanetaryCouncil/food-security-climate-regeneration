# Strategic Food Reserve and Food Independence

::: {.op-lede}
Britain grows just enough food in a normal year and stores none. For **about 4p a day each**, it could keep a national food reserve and rebuild the soil, water and seed every harvest depends on.
:::
:::

::: {.op-cols}
::: {.op-col}
## Where we are

### :i-people: The UK

[69.5 m]{.n} people^1^

[16.8 Mha]{.n} of farmland, 69% of UK land; 6.1 Mha can grow crops^2^

[0.09 ha]{.n} of cropland per person; France has ~0.28 ha^3^

[446,200]{.n} people work in farming; 40% of English farm holders are over 65^4^

[4.1 m]{.n} jobs in food, farm to café, adding £162bn a year^5^

[2×]{.n} the world average wheat yield: ~7.8 t a hectare^6^

### :i-wheat: What we grow, what we import

[60%]{.n} of the food we eat is grown here, by value; over 80% in the 1980s^7^

[101%]{.n} of the calories we need, in a normal year: a margin of 31 kcal a day each^8^

[83 · 56 · 14%]{.n} of our wheat · fresh vegetables · fresh fruit is home-grown^7^

[~52%]{.n} of UK wheat, barley and oats is fed to animals; livestock use 84% of farmland^9^

[2.39 Mt]{.n} of soya meal imported a year, ~1.1 Mt of protein, mostly for pigs and poultry^10^

[40%]{.n} of the fish we eat is UK-caught or farmed; 89% in 1975^11^

### :i-ship: Just in time

[~10 days]{.n} of food in the shops at any one time^12^

[39 days]{.n} of wheat use left in store at the pre-harvest low (1.5 Mt)^13^

[>50%]{.n} of the food we eat is chilled or frozen: no power, no cold^14^

[42 · 39%]{.n} of dairy and egg · meat imports cross the Dover–Calais strait^15^

[0]{.n} large ammonia plants since Billingham closed in 2023; all phosphate imported^16^

[904 kt]{.n} of fertiliser nitrogen used in 2025^17^

[$400→850]{.n} urea per tonne in 2026; up to 30% of traded fertiliser passes Hormuz^18^

[4 of 5]{.n} of the UK's worst harvests on record came this decade^19^

[0]{.n} national food stocks, and "no plans to create these"^20^
:::

::: {.op-col .op-do}
## What we can do

### :i-silo: STORE · a national food reserve

::: {.op-price}
**£10.33** per person a year · £718m · ~6.8 Mt of stock^21^
:::

[4.35 Mt]{.n} of milling wheat: ~10 months of bread at today's milling, ~84 days of everyone's calories^22^

[104 kt]{.n} no-cook pack for all 69.5 m: 3 days, ~2,000 kcal a day^23^

[90 days]{.n} of vitamin tablets (A, C, D, zinc) for everyone^24^

[7 kt]{.n} infant formula, medical foods, a 7-day pack for 5 m with special needs^25^

[90 m L]{.n} bottled water: 10 m people for 3 days; the rest by water-company plan^26^

[~1.2 Mt]{.n} rice 220 kt and oil 135 kt (~4 months), sugar 330 kt (~2 months), pulses, oats, milk powder, tinned fish^27^

[300 kt]{.n} of cereal seed, winter and spring^28^

[162 kt N]{.n} of fertiliser nitrogen as urea and UAN; never ammonium nitrate^29^

[460 kt]{.n} of soya meal: ~10 weeks of imports^30^

### :i-factory: Where, and who runs it

[8–10]{.n} port hubs at bulk-grain ports (Immingham, Tilbury, Liverpool, Avonmouth, Ipswich, Belfast), ~30 district stores, thousands of local points^31^

[£0.70bn]{.n} of new stores for 2 Mt of wheat; £3.69bn up front in all, over ~5 years^32^

[~65]{.n} staff in a lean agency (max 100), £33m a year with sensors and an independent inspectorate^33^

[~1,070]{.n} permanent jobs across stores, labs, haulage and local plans^34^

[£100m]{.n} a year for priority fuel, AdBlue and backup power at mills and depots^35^

### :i-sprout: GROW · soil, water, seed

::: {.op-price}
**£4.46** per person a year · £310m of new money^36^
:::

[50→500]{.n} soil-moisture stations, plus a free soil test for every field every 5 years (£35m)^37^

[£100m]{.n} a year for farm reservoirs that fill in winter (rounds are ~£15m today)^38^

[£125m]{.n} for UK seed multiplication, nutrient recycling and home-grown protein^39^

[£50m]{.n} to help farms through the switch to soil-building farming^40^
:::
:::

::: {.op-foot}
::: {.op-total}
**£14.79 a person a year, about 4p a day.** Defence costs ~£890.^41^ Range tested: £9.40 to £18.34; ~£12 if firms borrow at commercial rates; worst case £25.57.^42^
:::

::: {.op-act}
**Decide now:** a law with a minimum reserve, published release triggers and a power to direct food firms · ask the ONS how the stock counts as debt · trial the mills, local delivery and a dietitian-designed ration.^43^
:::

## Sources and working

::: {.rb-intro}

Models, research notes and the full evidence file: [github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration](https://github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration/tree/claude/focused-mendel-5qswh8).

::: {.refs}
1. **69.5 million people.** [P] ONS mid-2025 estimate ≈ 69.48 m, rounded. Used throughout as the per-person divisor. [ONS, population estimates](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/populationestimates)
2. **16.8 Mha of farmland, 69% of UK land; 6.1 Mha croppable.** [P] Utilised agricultural area, June 2025. [Defra, *Agriculture in the UK 2025*, ch. 2 (table 2.1)](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/agriculture-in-the-united-kingdom-2025/chapter-2-structure-of-industry)
3. **0.09 ha of cropland per person; France ~0.28 ha.** [E] Our arithmetic: FAO arable land totals ÷ population for each country. For scale, 0.09 ha ≈ 900 m², about three and a half doubles tennis courts.
4. **446,200 work in farming; 40% of English farm holders over 65, 5% under 35.** [P] [Defra, *Farming evidence pack* (Sep 2026)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/farming-evidence-pack-a-high-level-overview-of-the-uk-agricultural-industry)
5. **4.1 m jobs and £162bn a year in the food sector.** [P] Agri-food sector from farming through manufacturing, wholesale, retail and catering; gross value added £162.3bn (2024); 4.1 m jobs in Great Britain. [E] Our rough comparison: £162bn is ~6% of UK output but 4.1 m is ~12% of jobs, so output per job is about half the UK average; many jobs are part-time. [Defra, *Food statistics in your pocket*](https://gov.uk/government/statistics/food-statistics-pocketbook/food-statistics-in-your-pocket)
6. **~7.8 t/ha wheat, about twice the world average.** [P/E] UK average derived from AHDB's survey; world average from FAO. A single season varies: 2026 winter wheat was ~6.9 t/ha, ~11% below the five-year average. [AHDB, final harvest report (2026)](https://ahdb.org.uk/news/final-harvest-report-confirms-cereal-yields-are-below-historic-averages-across-the-uk)
7. **60% of food by value; over 80% in the 1980s; wheat 83%, fresh vegetables 56%, fresh fruit 14%.** [P] "Production to supply ratio", 2025. The 60% is by money value, so it moves with import prices; it is not a calorie share. The crop shares are by volume. [Defra, *Agriculture in the UK 2025*, ch. 14](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/agriculture-in-the-united-kingdom-2025/chapter-14-the-food-chain)
8. **101% of calories; 31 kcal per person per day.** [P] Domestic farm output against population need, assuming no waste. Defra notes the margin would shrink if imported feed stopped. 31 kcal is less than half a slice of bread (a medium slice is ~80–100 kcal: our comparison). [Defra, *Nutritional supply of agriculture in the UK 2025* (15 Oct 2026)](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/nutritional-supply-of-agriculture-in-the-uk-2025/nutritional-supply-of-agriculture-in-the-uk-2025)
9. **~52% of cereals to animal feed; livestock use 84% of farmland.** [P] Feed share from Defra cereal usage statistics (2023 data); the land share counts grazing plus feed crops, under Defra's stated assumptions, and livestock supply ~31% of farm calories. [Defra, *Agriculture in the UK 2025*, ch. 2](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/agriculture-in-the-united-kingdom-2025/chapter-2-structure-of-industry)
10. **2.39 Mt soya meal, ~1.1 Mt protein.** [S/E] Import tonnage from the AIC soya report (2024); protein is our arithmetic at ~46% crude protein.
11. **40% of fish UK-caught or farmed (2019); 89% in 1975.** [P] [Harrison et al. 2023, *Reviews in Fish Biology and Fisheries*](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/36032/1/s11160-023-09776-5.pdf)
12. **~10 days of food in the shops.** [S] The industry's usual estimate of retail stock, not a census. [Full Fact, the UK's food supply](https://fullfact.org/economy/uks-food-supply-hanging-balance/)
13. **1.514 Mt of wheat at the pre-harvest low ≈ 39 days.** [P/E] Commercial stocks at end of season; days are our arithmetic against total UK wheat use. [AHDB, end-of-season UK cereal supply and demand (Sep 2026)](https://ahdb.org.uk/end-of-season-uk-cereal-supply-and-demand-september-2026)
14. **More than half of food eaten is chilled or frozen.** [S] [Cold Chain Federation, policy strategy 2026](https://www.coldchainfederation.org.uk/policy-strategy-2026/)
15. **42% of dairy and egg imports, 39% of meat imports via the Short Straits** (Dover and Folkestone to Calais and Dunkirk). [P] [Defra, *UK Food Security Report 2024*, theme 3](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/united-kingdom-food-security-report-2024/united-kingdom-food-security-report-2024-theme-3-food-supply-chain-resilience)
16. **No large-scale ammonia production since Billingham closed in 2023; all phosphate imported.** [P] Ammonia: same Defra report as note 15. Phosphate: ~174 kt of phosphorus imported a year, under half used productively. [Lancaster University, UK Phosphorus Transformation Strategy (2022)](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/news/scientists-release-uk-roadmap-for-managing-key-ingredient-behind-all-the-food-we-eat)
17. **904 kt of fertiliser nitrogen, 2025.** [P] [Defra, British Survey of Fertiliser Practice 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aa3fb1dc5796a7a179c6728/BSFP_2025_Stats_Notice.pdf)
18. **Urea $400 → $850 a tonne (early 2026 to April); up to 30% of traded fertiliser through Hormuz.** [P] [WTO data blog (10 Jul 2026)](https://www.wto.org/english/blogs_e/data_blog_e/blog_dta_10jul26_451_e.htm); [FAO, chief economist on the Strait of Hormuz (2026)](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/fao-chief-economist-warns-of-severe-global-food-security-risks-from-disruption-to-strait-of-hormuz-trade-corridor/en). We could not confirm whether Hormuz was open in late October 2026.
19. **Four of the UK's five worst harvests on record this decade** (2020, 2024, 2025, 2026). [S] UK key-crop volume; 2026 is on track to be the smallest on record. [ECIU (Oct 2026)](https://eciu.net/media/press-releases/uk-harvest-even-worse-than-previously-thought-new-data)
20. **No national food stocks, "no plans to create these".** [P] [UK Parliament, written answer HL15829 (31 Mar 2026)](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-03-20/HL15829/)
21. **£10.33 per person a year, £718m, ~6.8 Mt.** [E] Our cost model: £375m carrying cost of compulsory commercial stocks (6.7 Mt, held by firms, cost passed through food prices: £5.40 a person) plus £343m for the public tranche and operations (taxes: £4.93). Carrying cost = finance + storage + rotation losses, not the purchase price. [`reserve_basket.py`](https://github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration/blob/claude/focused-mendel-5qswh8/research/model/reserve_basket.py)
22. **4 Mt food-grade wheat + 0.35 Mt high-protein wheat; ~10 months of bread; ~84 days of calories.** [E] 4 Mt ÷ UK mill intake (4.8 Mt a year, [UK Flour Millers](https://www.ukflourmillers.org/the-industry/annual-review)) ≈ 304 days, if mills have power, staff and the right mix. The strong wheat for today's sliced loaf lasts ~90 days; after that bread becomes heavier. As raw energy: 4 Mt × ~3.3 m kcal/t ÷ (69.5 m × 2,250 kcal) ≈ 84 days. Annual cost £182m. Same model as note 21.
23. **No-cook pack: 104 kt.** [E] 3 days × 69.5 m × ~500 g of crispbread, biscuits and oatcakes ≈ 104,000 t, ~2,000 kcal a day at 4 kcal/g; stored near where people live, rotated into schools, forces and food banks every ~2 years. £28m a year. Not yet designed by dietitians. Model as note 21.
24. **Vitamin tablets for 90 days.** [E] Vitamins A (as beta-carotene), C and D plus zinc, for 69.5 m. £19m a year. Model as note 21.
25. **Infant formula ~7 kt; medical foods; 7-day specialist pack for 5 m people.** [E] Formula held by manufacturers; specialist pack for people who cannot use the standard ration (allergies, coeliac disease, texture needs). £44m a year together. Model as note 21.
26. **90 m litres of bottled water.** [E] 10 m people × 3 days × 3 L. All 69.5 m would need ~625 m L, so the rest depends on water companies' emergency duties, which need a published, tested plan. £6m a year. Model as note 21.
27. **Imported staples ~1.2 Mt.** [E] Rice 220 kt (~4 months of UK use), vegetable oil 135 kt (~4 months of rapeseed-oil use), sugar 330 kt (~2 months), pulses 300 kt, oats 100 kt, skimmed milk powder 50 kt, tinned fish 40 kt. Each rotates at 4–16% of UK use a year, so it does not swamp the market. £81m a year. Model as note 21.
28. **300 kt of cereal seed.** [E] Winter 0.2 Mt + spring 0.1 Mt, kept separate. £44m a year. Reviewers note certified seed loses germination if held over, so a smaller cold vault plus paid options on seed-growing land may be cheaper; under review.
29. **162 kt of nitrogen.** [E] 200 kt protected urea (92 kt N) + 250 kt UAN-28 in heated tanks (70 kt N), ~18% of a year's use: a pre-season buffer. Solid ammonium nitrate, the Beirut chemical, is excluded on safety grounds. [HSE, ammonium nitrate and COMAH](https://www.hse.gov.uk/explosives/ammonium/comah.htm); model as note 21.
30. **460 kt of soya meal, ~10 weeks of imports.** [E] Rotated first-in-first-out through normal trade. £45m a year. Model as note 21.
31. **8–10 port hubs, ~30 district stores, thousands of local points.** [E] Hubs at bulk-grain ports, not Dover (a lorry port); district stores across all four nations, away from Humber and Wash flood zones; a statutory minimum in Northern Ireland in case ferries stop. [Research note 23](https://github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration/blob/claude/focused-mendel-5qswh8/research/notes/23-lean-open-agency-and-decentralised-network.md)
32. **£0.70bn of new stores; £3.69bn up front.** [E] 2 Mt of new wheat storage at ~£350/t, financed by store operators. Up front: public stock £0.72bn + firms' stock £2.24bn + new stores £0.70bn + sensors £0.02bn. Reviewers suggest turnkey silos may cost £400–500/t. Model as note 21.
33. **Agency of ~65, £33m a year.** [E] Staff £5.8m; innovation £8m; sensors and auto-samplers £2.9m; data platform and backups £4m; lab certificates £3m; open data £1.5m; independent inspectorate £8m. Every role has a lead and two deputies; two signatures per release. Model as note 21.
34. **~1,070 permanent jobs.** [E] Agency ~65, grain and food stores ~280, plus labs, haulage, local planning and security. Gross, not net of jobs displaced. Model as note 21.
35. **£100m a year for priority fuel, AdBlue and backup power.** [E] An allowance on a published priority list; not yet broken down into generators, litres and sites. Reviewers flag this as the line most in need of detail. Model as note 21.
36. **£4.46 per person a year, £310m.** [E] New money only. Existing farm support (£4.87bn, ~£70 a person) would gradually be aimed at resilience, but some of it is tax relief or signed contracts. [Defra, *Agriculture in the UK 2025*, ch. 10–11](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/agriculture-in-the-united-kingdom-2025/chapter-11-agri-environment); model as note 21.
37. **Soil-moisture stations from ~50 to ~500; free soil tests.** [P/E] Today's network: [COSMOS-UK, *ESSD* (2021)](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/13/1737/2021/essd-13-1737-2021.html). £35m a year including satellite moisture and a national soil-health baseline. Model as note 21.
38. **£100m a year for reservoirs.** [P/E] Current grant rounds £10–15m; the August 2026 round was £15m. [Defra Farming blog (Aug 2026)](https://defrafarming.blog.gov.uk/2026/08/15/new-drought-support-for-farmers/)
39. **£125m: seed £40m, nutrients £60m, protein £25m.** [E] Gene banks and UK seed multiplication; manure testing, phosphorus recovery and a green-ammonia pilot; dehulling and pulse mills. Model as note 21.
40. **£50m for the regenerative transition.** [P/E] Independent advice, farmer networks and bridging payments. Profit often improves after 3–5 years, but paying back the switch takes ~9 years without support. [WBCSD, *Closing the gap*](https://www.wbcsd.org/resources/closing-the-gap-an-analysis-of-the-costs-and-incentives-for-regenerative-agriculture-in-europe/)
41. **£14.79 a person a year; defence ~£890.** [P/E] £10.33 + £4.46 = £14.79 ÷ 365 ≈ 4p a day. Defence ~£62bn (2025/26) ÷ 69.5 m ≈ £890. [House of Commons Library, CBP-8175](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-8175/CBP-8175.pdf)
42. **Range £9.40–£25.57.** [E] Low: finance 3.5%, storage −10% (£9.40). Prices +30% (£11.35). Firms financing at 8% without a state facility (£11.92) or 10% (£12.73). Grain and fertiliser prices doubled (£13.73). Stress (£16.56); severe (£18.34); extreme (£25.57). If the ONS counts firms' stock as public, government debt rises by ~£2.2bn. Model as note 21.
43. **Decisions.** The law: emergency regulations under the Civil Contingencies Act lapse after 30 days, so a standing reserve needs its own Act ([Civil Contingencies Act 2004, Part 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/36/part/2)). No power to direct food firms today, and local planning found "insufficient" ([NAO, Sep 2026](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/resilience-of-the-food-supply-chain-to-disruptions/)). Comparable countries already hold reserves: Finland at least 6 months of bread grain; Switzerland 2–4 months of staples, held by firms by law ([réservesuisse](https://www.reservesuisse.ch/compulsory-stocks/?lang=en)). The full 22-page briefing sets out the case chapter by chapter: [`pdf/can-britain-feed-itself.pdf`](https://github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration/blob/claude/focused-mendel-5qswh8/pdf/can-britain-feed-itself.pdf)
