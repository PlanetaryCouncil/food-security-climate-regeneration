# Strategic Food Reserve and Food Independence

::: {.op-lede}
Food is the one thing nobody can live without. Britain is better placed to grow it than most countries: mild, rain all year, no dry season, broad flat fertile lowlands. Our farms can grow more than we eat. But every harvest relies on imported fertiliser, feed and energy, and there is no national food reserve if they fail.
:::

::: {.op-goal}
**Our aim:** grow a reliable surplus, and insure it with a national food reserve, for **about 4p a person a day**.
:::
:::

::: {.op-cols}
::: {.op-col}
## Where we are

### :i-people: The UK

[69.5 m]{.n} people to feed^1^

[16.8 m ha]{.n} of farmland, 69% of the UK. Only 6.1 m hectares can grow crops; the rest is mostly grass and hills^2^

[0.09 ha]{.n} of cropland per person: about three tennis courts. France has three times as much^3^

[446,200]{.n} people work on farms; 4 in 10 English farmers are over 65^4^

[Twice]{.n} the world average wheat harvest per hectare (about 7.8 tonnes)^5^

### :i-wheat: What we grow

[112%]{.n} of the calories the UK needs: what UK farms grew on average over the last 10 years (101% in 2025), before waste^6^

[83%]{.n} of the wheat we use is grown here; fresh vegetables 56%, fresh fruit only 14%^7^

[52%]{.n} of our wheat, barley and oats is fed to animals. Animals use 84% of farmland^8^

[~170 kcal]{.n} extra per person per day if animals got 10% less grain (our estimate)^9^

### :i-ship: How fragile it is

[2.39 Mt]{.n} of soya animal feed is imported each year, mostly for pigs and poultry^10^

[0]{.n} large UK plants making ammonia, the base of nitrogen fertiliser, since 2022. All phosphate is imported^11,12^

[Doubled]{.n} urea fertiliser price in early 2026 ($400 to $850 a tonne). Up to 30% of world fertiliser trade passes Hormuz^13^

[~10 days]{.n} of food in shops and their depots (industry estimate); 39 days of wheat in store just before harvest^14,15^

[Over half]{.n} of the food we eat is chilled or frozen: it must stay cold at every step, which takes power^16^

[42%]{.n} of imported dairy and eggs, and 39% of imported meat, cross the Channel at Dover^17^

[4 of 5]{.n} of the UK's smallest harvests on record are likely to fall in this decade^18^

[0]{.n} national food reserve: firms hold working stock only, and the government has "no plans" for one^19^
:::

::: {.op-col .op-do}
## What we can do

### :i-silo: STORE · the insurance policy

::: {.op-price}
**£10.33** a person a year (£718m) for ~6.8 m tonnes of food and farm supplies^20^
:::

[4.35 Mt]{.n} of bread wheat: flour for ~10 months at today's use, or ~84 days of everyone's calories^21^

[3 days]{.n} of ready-to-eat food (no cooking) for every person, ~2,000 kcal a day, stored locally^22^

[90 days]{.n} of vitamins and minerals for everyone, with doses for each age set by nutritionists^23^

[7,000 t]{.n} of baby milk, plus medical foods and a 7-day pack for 5 m people on special diets^24^

[90 m litres]{.n} of bottled water, on top of water firms' legal duty of 10 litres a person a day^25^

[4 months]{.n} of rice and cooking oil, 2 months of sugar, plus beans, oats, milk powder, tinned fish^26^

[1.2 Mt]{.n} for next year's harvest: seed (300,000 t), safe fertiliser (450,000 t), 10 weeks of soya feed (460,000 t)^27,28,29^

### :i-factory: Where it is kept, who runs it

[8 to 10]{.n} port stores (e.g. Immingham, Liverpool, Belfast), ~30 district stores, thousands of local points^30^

[£3.69bn]{.n} up front over ~5 years, mostly the food itself; £0.70bn of it builds new grain stores^31^

[~65 staff]{.n} in a small agency (never over 100), £33m a year with sensors and independent inspectors^32^

[~1,070]{.n} permanent jobs in stores, labs, transport and local planning^33^

[£100m]{.n} a year so mills and depots keep running: priority fuel, lorry fluid (AdBlue), generators^34^

### :i-sprout: GROW · build the surplus

::: {.op-price}
**£4.46** a person a year (£310m) of new money^35^
:::

[500]{.n} soil-moisture stations, up from 50, and a free soil test for every field every 5 years^36^

[£100m]{.n} a year for farm reservoirs that store winter rain for dry summers (today ~£15m a round)^37^

[£175m]{.n} for British seed, recycled nutrients, home-grown protein, and helping farms switch to soil-building methods^38,39^
:::
:::

::: {.op-foot}
::: {.op-total}
**Store and grow together: £14.79 a person a year, about 4p a day, under 2% of the defence budget.**^40^ Our model's central estimate; range £9.40 to £18.34, worst case tested £25.57.^41^
:::

::: {.op-act}
**First steps:** a law setting a minimum reserve, clear release rules and a power to direct food firms in a crisis · confirm how the stock counts in public debt · trial the mills, local delivery and the food pack.^42^

## Sources and working

::: {.rb-intro}
Every number on page 1 has an entry below: what it says, where it comes from, and how we did the arithmetic when it is ours. **[P]** official statistics or peer-reviewed research · **[S]** secondary or press source · **[E]** our estimate or model. Check any of it yourself, or paste these pages into an AI and ask it to. Models, research notes and the full evidence file: [github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration](https://github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration/tree/claude/focused-mendel-5qswh8).

::: {.refs}
1. **69.5 million people.** [P] ONS mid-2025 estimate ≈ 69.48 m, rounded. Used throughout as the per-person divisor. [ONS, population estimates](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/populationestimates)
2. **16.8 Mha of farmland, 69% of UK land; 6.1 Mha croppable.** [P] Utilised agricultural area, June 2025. [Defra, *Agriculture in the UK 2025*, ch. 2 (table 2.1)](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/agriculture-in-the-united-kingdom-2025/chapter-2-structure-of-industry)
3. **0.09 ha of cropland per person; France ~0.28 ha.** [E] Our arithmetic: FAO arable land totals ÷ population for each country. For scale, 0.09 ha ≈ 900 m², about three and a half doubles tennis courts.
4. **446,200 work in farming; 40% of English farm holders over 65, 5% under 35.** [P] [Defra, *Farming evidence pack* (Sep 2026)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/farming-evidence-pack-a-high-level-overview-of-the-uk-agricultural-industry)
5. **~7.8 t/ha wheat, about twice the world average; a mild, wet climate.** [P/E] UK average derived from AHDB's survey; world average from FAO. A single season varies: 2026 winter wheat was ~6.9 t/ha, ~11% below the five-year average ([AHDB, final harvest report (2026)](https://ahdb.org.uk/news/final-harvest-report-confirms-cereal-yields-are-below-historic-averages-across-the-uk)). Temperate oceanic climate, rain all year, no dry season ([Met Office, UK climate zones](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/climate/climate-explained/climate-zones)). About 42% of England's farmland is graded best and most versatile (Natural England land classification). [I] "Better placed than most countries" is our judgement from these facts; the limits are land per person (note 3), and weather that is becoming less reliable (note 18).
6. **101% of calories in 2025; 105% over five years.** [P] Farm output equal to 2,150 kcal per person a day against an average need of 2,119 (a margin of 31 kcal), before waste and processing losses; 105% on average over five years, 112% over ten. Defra notes the margin would shrink if imported feed stopped. 31 kcal is less than half a slice of bread (a medium slice is ~80–100 kcal: our comparison). [Defra, *Nutritional supply of agriculture in the UK 2025* (15 Oct 2026)](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/nutritional-supply-of-agriculture-in-the-uk-2025/nutritional-supply-of-agriculture-in-the-uk-2025)
7. **60% of food by value; over 80% in the 1980s; wheat 83%, fresh vegetables 56%, fresh fruit 14%.** [P] "Production to supply ratio", 2025. The 60% is by money value, so it moves with import prices; it is not a calorie share. The crop shares are by volume. [Defra, *Agriculture in the UK 2025*, ch. 14](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/agriculture-in-the-united-kingdom-2025/chapter-14-the-food-chain)
8. **~52% of cereals to animal feed; livestock use 84% of farmland.** [P] Feed share from Defra cereal usage statistics (2023 data); the land share counts grazing plus feed crops, under Defra's stated assumptions, and livestock supply ~31% of farm calories. [Defra, *Agriculture in the UK 2025*, ch. 2](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/agriculture-in-the-united-kingdom-2025/chapter-2-structure-of-industry)
9. **~170 kcal per person per day if animals got 10% less grain.** [E] Our arithmetic: UK livestock eat ~13.4 Mt of cereals a year ([AHDB cereal supply and demand, 2025/26](https://ahdb.org.uk/end-of-season-uk-cereal-supply-and-demand-september-2026)). 10% is 1.34 Mt × ~3.3 m kcal a tonne ÷ 69.5 m people ÷ 365 ≈ 174 kcal, about five times the 2025 margin of 31 kcal. Grain is not a whole diet, and less feed means less meat, milk and eggs. Our grain model shows that with feed at 90% of today, normal harvests carry the UK through three years without imports and without extra reserve ([`cereal_balance.py`](https://github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration/blob/claude/focused-mendel-5qswh8/research/model/cereal_balance.py)).
10. **2.39 Mt soya meal, ~1.1 Mt protein.** [S/E] Import tonnage from the AIC soya report (2024); protein is our arithmetic at ~46% crude protein.
11. **No large-scale UK ammonia production; all phosphate imported.** [P] CF Fertilisers idled the Billingham ammonia plant in August 2022 and proposed permanent closure in July 2023; fertiliser is still made there from imported ammonia ([CF Industries (Jul 2023)](https://cfindustries.com/newsroom/2023/billingham-ammonia-plant)). Also Defra's report in note 17. Phosphate: ~174 kt of phosphorus imported a year, under half used productively. [Lancaster University, UK Phosphorus Transformation Strategy (2022)](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/news/scientists-release-uk-roadmap-for-managing-key-ingredient-behind-all-the-food-we-eat)
12. **904 kt of fertiliser nitrogen, 2025.** [P] [Defra, British Survey of Fertiliser Practice 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aa3fb1dc5796a7a179c6728/BSFP_2025_Stats_Notice.pdf)
13. **Urea $400 → $850 a tonne (early 2026 to April); up to 30% of traded fertiliser through Hormuz.** [P] [WTO data blog (10 Jul 2026)](https://www.wto.org/english/blogs_e/data_blog_e/blog_dta_10jul26_451_e.htm); [FAO, chief economist on the Strait of Hormuz (2026)](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/fao-chief-economist-warns-of-severe-global-food-security-risks-from-disruption-to-strait-of-hormuz-trade-corridor/en). We could not confirm whether Hormuz was open in late October 2026.
14. **~10 days of food in the shops.** [S] The industry's rule of thumb for retail stock, not a measured national inventory; it leaves out farm, manufacturer and wholesale stocks, and varies by food. [Full Fact, the UK's food supply](https://fullfact.org/economy/uks-food-supply-hanging-balance/)
15. **1.514 Mt of wheat at the pre-harvest low ≈ 39 days.** [P/E] Commercial stocks at end of season; days are our arithmetic against total UK wheat use. [AHDB, end-of-season UK cereal supply and demand (Sep 2026)](https://ahdb.org.uk/end-of-season-uk-cereal-supply-and-demand-september-2026)
16. **More than half of food eaten is chilled or frozen.** [S] [Cold Chain Federation, policy strategy 2026](https://www.coldchainfederation.org.uk/policy-strategy-2026/)
17. **42% of dairy and egg imports, 39% of meat imports via the Short Straits** (Dover and Folkestone to Calais and Dunkirk). [P] [Defra, *UK Food Security Report 2024*, theme 3](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/united-kingdom-food-security-report-2024/united-kingdom-food-security-report-2024-theme-3-food-supply-chain-resilience)
18. **Four of the UK's five worst harvests on record likely this decade** (2020, 2024, 2025, 2026). [S] ECIU's analysis of key-crop volume from AHDB and Defra data, before final 2026 figures (due December); it expects 2026 to be the smallest on record. [ECIU (Oct 2026)](https://eciu.net/media/press-releases/uk-harvest-even-worse-than-previously-thought-new-data)
19. **No national strategic food reserve, "no plans to create these".** [P] Firms hold working stock (note 15); the government relies on commercial supply chains. [UK Parliament, written answer HL15829 (31 Mar 2026)](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-03-20/HL15829/)
20. **£10.33 per person a year, £718m, ~6.8 Mt.** [E] Our cost model: £375m carrying cost of compulsory commercial stocks (6.7 Mt, held by firms, cost passed through food prices: £5.40 a person) plus £343m for the public tranche and operations (taxes: £4.93). Carrying cost = finance + storage + rotation losses, not the purchase price. [`reserve_basket.py`](https://github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration/blob/claude/focused-mendel-5qswh8/research/model/reserve_basket.py)
21. **4 Mt food-grade wheat + 0.35 Mt high-protein wheat; ~10 months of bread; ~84 days of calories.** [E] 4 Mt ÷ UK mill intake (4.8 Mt a year, [UK Flour Millers](https://www.ukflourmillers.org/the-industry/annual-review)) ≈ 304 days, if mills have power, staff and the right mix. The strong wheat for today's sliced loaf lasts ~90 days; after that bread becomes heavier. As raw energy: 4 Mt × ~3.3 m kcal/t ÷ (69.5 m × 2,250 kcal) ≈ 84 days. Annual cost £182m. Same model as note 20.
22. **No-cook pack: 104 kt.** [E] 3 days × 69.5 m × ~500 g of crispbread, biscuits and oatcakes ≈ 104,000 t, ~2,000 kcal a day at 4 kcal/g; stored near where people live, rotated into schools, forces and food banks every ~2 years. £28m a year. Not yet designed by dietitians. Model as note 20.
23. **90 days of supplements.** [E] The model budgets vitamins A (as beta-carotene), C and D plus zinc for 69.5 m, £19m a year. One formula will not suit everyone: high-dose beta-carotene raised lung-cancer risk in smokers in two large trials ([ATBC, *NEJM* 1994](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199404143301501); [CARET, *NEJM* 1996](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199605023341802)), and children and pregnancy need their own doses. Formulas must be set by public-health nutritionists. Model as note 20.
24. **Infant formula ~7 kt; medical foods; 7-day specialist pack for 5 m people.** [E] Formula held by manufacturers; specialist pack for people who cannot use the standard ration (allergies, coeliac disease, texture needs). £44m a year together. Model as note 20.
25. **90 m litres of bottled water.** [E] 10 m people × 3 days × 3 L. All 69.5 m would need ~625 m L. Under the Security and Emergency Measures Direction, water companies must already plan to supply 10 L a person a day within 24 hours if pipes fail, rising to 20 L after 5 days ([DWI, Security and emergencies](https://www.dwi.gov.uk/semd); [United Utilities, drought plan annex citing SEMD](https://www.unitedutilities.com/globalassets/z_corporate-site/about-us-pdfs/final-drought-plan-2022/h-non-public-water-supply.pdf)). The bottled water is a small extra buffer, not national water security. £6m a year. Model as note 20.
26. **Imported staples ~1.2 Mt.** [E] Rice 220 kt (~4 months of UK use), vegetable oil 135 kt (~4 months of rapeseed-oil use), sugar 330 kt (~2 months), pulses 300 kt, oats 100 kt, skimmed milk powder 50 kt, tinned fish 40 kt. Each rotates at 4–16% of UK use a year, so it does not swamp the market. £81m a year. Model as note 20.
27. **300 kt of cereal seed.** [E] Winter 0.2 Mt + spring 0.1 Mt, kept separate. £44m a year. Reviewers note certified seed loses germination if held over, so a smaller cold vault plus paid options on seed-growing land may be cheaper; under review.
28. **162 kt of nitrogen.** [E] 200 kt protected urea (92 kt N) + 250 kt UAN-28 in heated tanks (70 kt N), ~18% of a year's use: a pre-season buffer. Solid ammonium nitrate, the Beirut chemical, is excluded on safety grounds. [HSE, ammonium nitrate and COMAH](https://www.hse.gov.uk/explosives/ammonium/comah.htm); model as note 20.
29. **460 kt of soya meal, ~10 weeks of imports.** [E] Rotated first-in-first-out through normal trade. £45m a year. Model as note 20.
30. **8–10 port hubs, ~30 district stores, thousands of local points.** [E] Hubs at bulk-grain ports, not Dover (a lorry port); district stores across all four nations, away from Humber and Wash flood zones; a statutory minimum in Northern Ireland in case ferries stop. [Research note 22](https://github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration/blob/claude/focused-mendel-5qswh8/research/notes/23-lean-open-agency-and-decentralised-network.md)
31. **£0.70bn of new stores; £3.69bn up front.** [E] 2 Mt of new wheat storage at ~£350/t, financed by store operators. Up front: public stock £0.72bn + firms' stock £2.24bn + new stores £0.70bn + sensors £0.02bn. Reviewers suggest turnkey silos may cost £400–500/t. Model as note 20.
32. **Agency of ~65, £33m a year.** [E] Staff £5.8m; innovation £8m; sensors and auto-samplers £2.9m; data platform and backups £4m; lab certificates £3m; open data £1.5m; independent inspectorate £8m. Every role has a lead and two deputies; two signatures per release. Model as note 20.
33. **~1,070 permanent jobs.** [E] Agency ~65, grain and food stores ~280, plus labs, haulage, local planning and security. Gross, not net of jobs displaced. Model as note 20.
34. **£100m a year for priority fuel, AdBlue and backup power.** [E] An allowance on a published priority list; not yet broken down into generators, litres and sites. Reviewers flag this as the line most in need of detail. Model as note 20.
35. **£4.46 per person a year, £310m.** [E] New money only. Existing farm support (£4.87bn, ~£70 a person) would gradually be aimed at resilience, but some of it is tax relief or signed contracts. [Defra, *Agriculture in the UK 2025*, ch. 10–11](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/agriculture-in-the-united-kingdom-2025/chapter-11-agri-environment); model as note 20.
36. **Soil-moisture stations from ~50 to ~500; free soil tests.** [P/E] Today's network: [COSMOS-UK, *ESSD* (2021)](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/13/1737/2021/essd-13-1737-2021.html). £35m a year including satellite moisture and a national soil-health baseline. Model as note 20.
37. **£100m a year for reservoirs.** [P/E] Current grant rounds £10–15m; the August 2026 round was £15m. [Defra Farming blog (Aug 2026)](https://defrafarming.blog.gov.uk/2026/08/15/new-drought-support-for-farmers/)
38. **£125m: seed £40m, nutrients £60m, protein £25m.** [E] Gene banks and UK seed multiplication; manure testing, phosphorus recovery and a green-ammonia pilot; dehulling and pulse mills. Model as note 20.
39. **£50m for the regenerative transition.** [P/E] Independent advice, farmer networks and bridging payments. Profit often improves after 3–5 years, but paying back the switch takes ~9 years without support. [WBCSD, *Closing the gap*](https://www.wbcsd.org/resources/closing-the-gap-an-analysis-of-the-costs-and-incentives-for-regenerative-agriculture-in-europe/)
40. **£14.79 a person a year; under 2% of defence.** [P/E] £10.33 + £4.46 = £14.79 ÷ 365 ≈ 4p a day. In total £14.79 × 69.5 m ≈ £1.03bn a year, 1.7% of Ministry of Defence spending of ~£62bn (2025/26; the departmental measure, not NATO's wider one), which is ~£890 a person. [House of Commons Library, CBP-8175](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-8175/CBP-8175.pdf)
41. **Range £9.40 to £25.57.** [E] These are annual carrying costs, including finance; the £3.69bn up front (note 31) is financed within them, not added on top. Low: finance 3.5%, storage −10% (£9.40). Prices +30% (£11.35). Firms financing at 8% without a state facility (£11.92) or 10% (£12.73). Grain and fertiliser prices doubled (£13.73). Stress (£16.56); severe (£18.34); extreme (£25.57). If the ONS counts firms' stock as public, government debt rises by ~£2.2bn. Model as note 20.
42. **Decisions.** The law: emergency regulations under the Civil Contingencies Act lapse after 30 days, so a standing reserve needs its own Act ([Civil Contingencies Act 2004, Part 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/36/part/2)). No power to direct food firms today, and local planning found "insufficient" ([NAO, Sep 2026](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/resilience-of-the-food-supply-chain-to-disruptions/)). Comparable countries already hold reserves: Finland at least 6 months of bread grain; Switzerland 2–4 months of staples, held by firms by law ([réservesuisse](https://www.reservesuisse.ch/compulsory-stocks/?lang=en)). The full 22-page briefing sets out the case chapter by chapter: [`pdf/can-britain-feed-itself.pdf`](https://github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration/blob/claude/focused-mendel-5qswh8/pdf/can-britain-feed-itself.pdf)
