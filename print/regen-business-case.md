# Farming That Rebuilds Soil

::: {.op-lede}
UK farms spend **£3.75bn a year on fertiliser, fuel and sprays: a sum equal to 45% of farming's total income.** Much of it is made from imported gas. The public pays a second bill in polluted water, worn-out soil and dirty air. Farming that rebuilds soil cuts both bills, and in a modelled UK farm it pays back by year six.
:::

::: {.op-goal}
**Our aim:** help farms make the switch, so British food depends less on imported inputs and more on healthy soil.
:::
:::

::: {.op-cols}
::: {.op-col}
## Two bills

### :i-pound: Bill 1: what farmers pay (2025)

[£1,779m]{.n} on fertiliser, made from gas; the UK no longer makes ammonia at scale^1,2^

[£1,047m]{.n} on fuel. Ploughing uses ~57 litres a hectare; direct drilling ~9^1,3^

[£925m]{.n} on sprays (plant protection products)^1^

[45%]{.n} these three inputs equal 45% of total farm income (£8.4bn) (our arithmetic)^1,4^

### :i-drop: Bill 2: what everyone else pays

[£0.9–1.4bn]{.n} a year: the cost of degraded soil in England and Wales^5^

[14.3%]{.n} of England's rivers, lakes and estuaries in good ecological health; farm nutrients are among the pressures^6^

[61%]{.n} of the phosphorus in Lough Neagh comes from farming. The lough supplies over 40% of Northern Ireland's drinking water; treatment cost ~£28m in 2022–23^7,8^

[88.7%]{.n} of UK ammonia emissions come from farming; ammonia helps form fine-particle air pollution^9^
:::

::: {.op-col .op-do}
## What changes

### :i-sprout: On the field

[Up to 40%]{.n} less nitrate leaching into water from deep-rooting cover crops^10^

[−84%]{.n} fuel when direct drilling replaces the plough (57 to 9 litres a hectare)^3^

[+25%]{.n} topsoil carbon in rested fields at Rothamsted over 28 years (0.98% to 1.23%), then a plateau^11^

[£630m]{.n} a year: insect pollination of UK fruit, vegetables and oilseed rape. Field margins and hedges feed pollinators^12^

### :i-chart: Does it pay?

[+31%]{.n} farm net margin vs conventional by year six (−10% in year one), modelled 810 ha arable farm, including payments, carbon income and premiums^13^

[−64%]{.n} the catch: the food-only margin in early years, with yields still 18% lower by year six. Payments bridge the gap^13^

[3 to 5 years]{.n} until common regenerative practices beat conventional on profit, across European farms of all sizes^14^

[£73 · £163]{.n} per hectare a year paid today for no-till · summer cover crops (SFI)^15^
:::
:::

::: {.op-faq}
::: {.faq}
**Why is this security policy?** Every tonne of fertiliser and litre of diesel a farm no longer needs is one less exposed to gas prices and shipping routes. In 2026 urea prices doubled within months.^16^
:::

::: {.faq}
**What is the catch?** The switch costs money for 3 to 6 years, and yields can stay lower. Someone has to fund that bridge: today's SFI payments, the food industry, or a dedicated transition fund.^13,15^
:::

::: {.faq}
**What do we not claim?** That it makes food more nutritious, or creates more jobs overall: the evidence is weak or mixed. That it stores carbon without limit: soil carbon rises, then levels off.^11^
:::
:::

::: {.op-foot}
::: {.op-total}
**The case in one line:** cut a £3.75bn input bill and a hidden public bill, and pay farms through the 3 to 6 lean years.
:::

**Read with:** our one-page plan for a UK food reserve, which funds the switch through its GROW pillar.^17^

::: {.refs}
1. **Farm input costs, 2025: fertiliser £1,779m, fuel £1,047m, plant protection £925m.** [P] Intermediate consumption, UK. [Defra, *Agriculture in the UK 2025*, ch. 4 accounts](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/agriculture-in-the-united-kingdom-2025/chapter-4-accounts)
2. **No large-scale UK ammonia production.** [P] CF Fertilisers idled the Billingham ammonia plant in August 2022 and proposed permanent closure in July 2023. [CF Industries (Jul 2023)](https://cfindustries.com/newsroom/2023/billingham-ammonia-plant)
3. **Ploughing ~57 litres a hectare; direct drilling ~9.** [S] Machinery consultant's figures. 9 ÷ 57 ≈ 16%, so ~84% less fuel (our arithmetic). [Farmers Weekly, direct drilling helps slash establishment costs](https://www.fwi.co.uk/arable/direct-drilling-helps-slash-establishment-costs)
4. **Total income from farming £8.4bn (2025).** [P/E] [Defra, *Agriculture in the UK 2025*, summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/agriculture-in-the-united-kingdom-2025/summary). (£1,779m + £1,047m + £925m) = £3,751m ÷ £8,400m ≈ 45%. Income is what is left after all costs, so this compares inputs with farmers' earnings, not with turnover.
5. **Soil degradation £0.9–1.4bn a year.** [P] England and Wales, 2015 study. [Graves et al. 2015, *Ecological Economics* 119](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolec/v119y2015icp399-413.html)
6. **14.3% of England's surface waters in good ecological status.** [S] Environment Agency classifications of 6 August 2026, reported by [Envirotec (Aug 2026)](https://envirotecmagazine.com/2026/08/11/just-14-of-englands-surface-waters-achieve-good-ecological-status-latest-ea-figures/). Farming is one pressure among several, including sewage.
7. **61% of Lough Neagh's phosphorus from farming.** [S] NEW Harmonica model (AFBI and universities), 2021 data; 29% from wastewater. [FactCheckNI](https://factcheckni.org/articles/lough-neagh-crisis-does-most-of-the-phosphorus-in-the-lough-come-from-agriculture/)
8. **Over 40% of Northern Ireland's drinking water; ~£28m treatment, 2022–23.** [S] [Al Jazeera (Nov 2023)](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2023/11/6/why-is-lough-neagh-the-uks-largest-freshwater-lake-turning-green). The £28m is the cost of treating water drawn from the lough, not all of it caused by farming.
9. **88.7% of UK ammonia emissions from farming (2024).** [P] Ammonia reacts in air to form fine particles. [National Atmospheric Emissions Inventory, UK agriculture ammonia report 1990–2024](https://naei.energysecurity.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-08/UK_Agriculture_Ammonia_Emission_Report_1990-2024_08.07.2026.pdf)
10. **Up to 40% less nitrate leaching.** [S] Deep-rooting cover crops such as fodder radish; guidance, not a single trial; early-drilled covers work best. [AHDB, cover crops reduce nutrient losses](https://ahdb.org.uk/knowledge-library/cover-crops-reduce-nutrient-losses-and-leaching)
11. **Topsoil carbon 0.98% → 1.23% over 28 years (+25%, our arithmetic).** [P] Grass–clover ley rotation on a sandy loam; then a plateau; continuous arable declined. [Johnston et al. 2017, Rothamsted](https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/item/8v42x/changes-in-soil-organic-matter-over-70-years-in-continuous-arable-and-ley-arable-rotations-on-a-sandy-loam-soil-in-england)
12. **Insect pollination worth ~£630m a year to UK crops.** [P] Defra written answer, 19 September 2025; fruit, vegetables and oilseed rape only, so narrower than older ~£690m estimates. [Westminster Brief, Agriculture: pollinators](https://westminsterbrief.co.uk/archive/pq/76874)
13. **Savills Virtual Farm.** [M] A modelled 810 ha top-25% East Midlands arable farm, 2024 economics. Overall net margin including SFI, carbon and regenerative premiums: 10% below conventional in year one, 31% above by year six. Food-production margin alone falls 64% in the early years; yields start 26% lower and are still 18% lower by year six. A model of one well-run farm, not a national average. [Savills (Jun 2024)](https://www.savills.co.uk/research_articles/229130/362979-0)
14. **Profit positive after 3 to 5 years.** [M] Six common regenerative practices, crops in scope, European farms; driven by projected yield gains and lower costs, strongest on farms over 55 ha. [WBCSD, *Closing the gap* (Mar 2025)](https://www.wbcsd.org/resources/closing-the-gap-an-analysis-of-the-costs-and-incentives-for-regenerative-agriculture-in-europe/)
15. **SFI payments: £73/ha no-till; £163/ha summer cover crop.** [P] Sustainable Farming Incentive actions SOH1 and SOH3, 3-year agreements. [GOV.UK, SOH1](https://www.gov.uk/find-funding-for-land-or-farms/soh1-no-till-farming); [GOV.UK, SOH3](https://gov.uk/find-funding-for-land-or-farms/soh3-multi-species-summer-sown-cover-crop)
16. **Urea $400 → $850 a tonne, early 2026 to April.** [P] [WTO data blog (10 Jul 2026)](https://www.wto.org/english/blogs_e/data_blog_e/blog_dta_10jul26_451_e.htm)
17. **The food reserve one-pager.** Strategic Food Reserve and Food Independence, with its GROW pillar (£310m a year, including £50m to bridge the switch). [`pdf/uk-food-reserve-at-a-glance.pdf`](https://github.com/planetarycouncil/food-security-climate-regeneration/blob/claude/focused-mendel-5qswh8/pdf/uk-food-reserve-at-a-glance.pdf)
